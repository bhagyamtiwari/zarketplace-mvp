# DRAFT FOR COUNSEL REVIEW — NOT FOR USE

**Status:** unreviewed draft. Do not publish, do not present to any vendor, and
do not rely on any statement in it until counsel has settled the open questions
in `GST_REVIEW_BRIEF.md`. Several clauses below assume answers we do not yet
have.

**Prepared:** 31 August 2026
**Counterparties:** zarketplace (the "Company") and an individual selling an
item to it (the "Vendor")

---

## 1. What this is

The Company **buys** the Vendor's item outright and later resells it under its
own GST registration. The Vendor is a **supplier of goods** to the Company.

The Vendor is not:

- an agent of the Company
- a seller on a marketplace
- a party to any contract with the person who eventually buys the item

There are two separate transactions. The Company's purchase from the Vendor is
one. The Company's sale to its own customer is another. Neither is conditional
on the other, and the Vendor is a party only to the first.

## 2. The acquisition price

**2.1** The Vendor states the amount they want for the item. The Company
responds with an **acquisition price** — a fixed rupee amount the Company will
pay for the item.

**2.2** The acquisition price is fixed at the moment the Vendor accepts it,
which is **before the item is listed and before any resale price exists**. It
is not a share, proportion or percentage of anything.

**2.3** The acquisition price does not change. It is not adjusted for what the
item is later sold for, whether it sells quickly or slowly, whether it sells at
a discount, or whether it sells at all.

**2.4** The Vendor has no right to be told, consulted on, or to approve the
price at which the Company resells the item, and no visibility into it.

## 3. When the item moves, and when title passes

**3.1** The item remains in the Vendor's possession until the Company has sold
it. The Company then issues a prepaid courier label at its own cost.

**3.2** The Vendor must hand the item to the courier within the period stated
when they accepted the acquisition price (**5 days**, subject to §9).

**3.3** **Title and risk pass to the Company on acceptance at its hub**, not on
dispatch and not on the Company's own onward sale.

**3.4** Before acceptance, risk in transit sits with the Company as the party
that arranged and paid for carriage. *(Open: counsel to confirm this allocation
is consistent with §3.3 and with the Sale of Goods Act 1930.)*

## 4. Acceptance, and refusal

**4.1** On arrival the Company checks the item against the Vendor's own listing
and photographs, and against the condition the Vendor described. **This is a
match check. The Company does not authenticate items** and does not represent
to anyone that it has done so.

**4.2** If the item matches, the Company accepts it into its inventory and the
acquisition price becomes payable.

**4.3** If it does not match, the Company may refuse it. No acquisition price
is payable for a refused item.

**4.4** On refusal the Company notifies the Vendor, states the reason, and
offers to return the item **at the Vendor's cost**.

**4.5** If the Vendor does not claim a refused item **within 60 days** of that
notice, the Company may donate or dispose of it, and the Vendor forfeits any
claim to the item or its value. *(Open: counsel to confirm 60 days and this
forfeiture are enforceable against a consumer counterparty, and whether notice
must take a particular form.)*

## 5. Payment

**5.1** The Company pays the acquisition price after it accepts the item.

**5.2** That payment is made by the Company from its own funds. It is **not**
made out of, conditional on, or timed to any payment the Company receives from
its own customer. The Vendor is paid whether or not the Company's customer has
paid, and whether or not that sale later completes.

**5.3** The Vendor supplies payout details and is responsible for their
accuracy. The Company may require a PAN before payment.

## 6. What the Vendor represents

The Vendor represents that, to the best of their knowledge:

- the item is genuine and not counterfeit
- they own it and may lawfully sell it
- the description, photographs and stated condition are accurate
- all known flaws are disclosed

## 7. Risk the Company takes on

From acceptance, the Company bears **all** resale risk: that the item does not
sell, sells for less than expected, is returned by its customer, or is damaged
or lost in its possession.

**None of this is passed back to the Vendor.** The Company has no right to
recover an acquisition price it has already paid because a resale went badly.
*(This is deliberate and load-bearing: a clawback would make the Company look
like an intermediary taking a position on someone else's sale.)*

## 8. No customer relationship

**8.1** The Vendor's identity is not disclosed to the Company's customers. No
name, no profile, no attribution, no rating.

**8.2** There is no channel between the Vendor and any customer, and neither is
told the other exists.

**8.3** The Vendor acquires no rights over the Company's customers and no claim
to any part of what a customer pays.

## 9. Changes

The Company may change the terms applying to **future** acquisitions. An
acquisition price already accepted is not affected by any later change.

## 10. Termination

Either party may stop transacting at any time. Obligations attaching to an item
already accepted survive.

## Notes for counsel

1. §2.2 (price fixed before resale price is known) and §7 (no clawback) are the
   two clauses doing the work of distinguishing a purchase from a facilitated
   sale. Please pressure-test both.
2. §4.1 disclaims authentication. Consider whether that disclaimer survives
   contact with the Consumer Protection Act 2019 given the Company **is** the
   seller to the end customer.
3. §4.5 forfeiture: enforceability against a consumer, and notice requirements.
4. Whether this should be a standing supply agreement or a per-item contract
   formed on each acceptance. Currently drafted as the latter.
5. Whether anything here is affected by the Company's classification under
   GST §52 or Income Tax §194-O. See `GST_REVIEW_BRIEF.md`.
